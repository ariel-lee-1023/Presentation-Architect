## Recovery and visual verification

The supplied Grid Systems Markdown contains severe bilingual/column noise and inflated text volume. Its PDF was used for bounded text recovery, including English-side clips, and construction examples were rendered. The PowerPoint essay, Williams, Reynolds and parts of Lupton also benefited from PDF text or visual checks. A successful PDF extraction does not mean its text order or OCR is perfect; unreliable detail was omitted or checked against the image.

## Reading accounting limits

The initial read helper recorded requested ranges and estimated tokens emitted, not exact tokens successfully displayed. Several oversized tool responses were truncated, including early Resonate, Storytelling with Data, Envisioning Information, the first bilingual Grid batch and a Lupton specimen-heavy range. Later bounded reads and PDF checks recovered selected needed claims. The raw request logs are retained with an explicit upper-bound qualification; they must not be described as proof that every requested line was read. TOC/search output and visual-image tokens are not comprehensively included in the numerical tally.

`reading-ledger.json` adds retrospective audit questions and reference dispositions to the request log so the structural reading-audit tool can summarize it. Those annotations are not contemporaneous experimental records. `reading-report.json` measures requested source spans and emitted token estimates; it does not establish semantic completeness. PDF page requests remain in their own ledger rather than being misleadingly counted as full Markdown-line coverage.

## Budget decisions

The core has six substantive sections and ten routed sources, giving a planning target of 4,050 tokens and a hard cap of 4,500. Reference targets use each book's detected contents structure, not the number of paragraphs written. The references are shorter than the provisional formula targets because repeated examples, galleries, historical anecdotes, exercises and catalogs were compressed or excluded as indicated above. Targets are not floors; no filler was added to reach them.

The two existing budget tools use heuristic text/technical classification and differ on the Knaflic file. This does not affect the outcome: every file is below either relevant cap. The extraction pipeline's technical mode retained layout-sensitive material; it does not imply that every reference must be classified as a mathematical/code-heavy text. Both actual tool reports are preserved without changing the metatool to conceal this distinction.

## Attribution and synthesis boundaries

Named source frameworks remain with their originating or explicitly credited author: Resonate's Big Idea and audience model; slide:ology's diagram families and data rules; Knaflic's six-step workflow; Cairo's Visualization Wheel; Tufte's layers and micro/macro readings; Müller-Brockmann's construction; Williams's four principles and type relationships; Lupton's hierarchy and reading; Reynolds's restrained live encounter. Reynolds's SUCCESs discussion credits Chip and Dan Heath. His use of smallest effective difference is identified as a borrowing from Tufte's Visual Explanations, which was not an additional supplied book.

Slide production workflows, density arbitration, user constraint handling and combined design decisions are labeled or expressed as Presentation Architect synthesis. The sources are not represented as agreeing with every synthesized rule. The 2003 PowerPoint essay's internal identity takes precedence over its misleading filename. Historical data and software advice are not asserted as current facts.
